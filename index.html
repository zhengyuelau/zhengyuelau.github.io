Wrist-worn sensor enclosure: designed for injection moulding

Personal project · CAD in NX / SolidWorks · SLA-printed prototypes

Redesigned a 38 mm wearable sensor housing from a CNC-machined prototype into a three-part, injection-mouldable design, reducing mass and part count while keeping IP67 sealing.

The problem

The original prototype was machined from aluminium, used four screws and a gasket, and could not be produced affordably above a few hundred units. The brief was a waterproof housing under 12 g that a user could open once to replace the battery.

My role

Sole designer: concept, CAD, tolerance analysis, prototyping and testing.

Key decisions

DecisionWhy
PC-ABS shell with ultrasonic-welded backRemoved screws, cut assembly time, gave a reliable seal
1.2 mm nominal wall with ribs at the strap lugsKept mass down while meeting the lug pull-test load
Snap-fit battery door with captive silicone sealAllowed tool-free user servicing
Single parting line along the side edgeAvoided witness lines on the top face

Image: FEA plot of the strap lug under 50 N

Iteration

Version 1 cracked at the snap-fit root. Version 2 added a fillet and increased the hook engagement, and passed 50 open/close cycles.

What I would do next

  • Run mould-flow analysis for gate location and weld lines
  • Trial a second material for scratch resistance
